Practice sets are embedded from their publisher's own build (HoodaMath, CrazyGames, Scratch, or the developer's own server). Audio plays through Spotify and SoundCloud's official embeds. When you press play, your browser talks to that company directly.

What needs legal review

These documents were drafted by reading the site's own code and configuration. They are not the product of legal advice, and four points genuinely need a lawyer admitted in Western Australia before you rely on them:

  1. Whether the operator is an "APP entity". The Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) applies to most businesses with turnover above the threshold, and to some below it. Which side this falls on is a question of fact, not of wording.
  2. Whether the Copyright Act safe harbour applies. Section 115A of the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth) gives qualifying service providers protection against authorisation liability, but only if the conditions are met — including a nominated agent and a compliant takedown scheme. The site does not currently assert that protection, and this document does not pretend it does.
  3. Whether linking and framing is authorisation of infringement. Australian law on authorisation is fact-specific. The site's position is that it provides links and frames only, that it holds no files, and that it takes notices seriously — but the boundary is a matter for advice, not a disclaimer.
  4. Whether the liability cap is enforceable against a consumer. The Australian Consumer Law voids terms that attempt to exclude guarantees it imposes. The cap in the liability page is drafted to sit inside that limit, not around it.
A disclaimer does not create immunity. Nothing in these pages removes liability for fraud, for misleading conduct under the Australian Consumer Law, or for anything else the law does not allow to be excluded. An accepted checkbox is evidence of a decision; it is not a shield.
